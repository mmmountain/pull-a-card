Landmarks

  • No sharp or flat between E–F and B–C. Every other pair of natural notes has one fret between them.
  • Fret 12 is the open string an octave up. Frets 13–15 repeat frets 1–3.
  • Octave shape: two strings up, two frets up. When the jump crosses onto the B string, go three frets up.
  • In standard tuning, fret 5 matches the next string's open note, except on the G string, where it's fret 4.
  • Low E and high e have the same note names at every fret, so learn five strings and you have six.

Order of sharps and flats

Sharps always arrive in this order: F♯ C♯ G♯ D♯ A♯ E♯ B♯ (Father Charles Goes Down And Ends Battle).

Flats are the same letters reversed: B♭ E♭ A♭ D♭ G♭ C♭ F♭ (Battle Ends And Down Goes Charles' Father).

A key with 3 sharps has the first three: F♯ C♯ G♯. A key with 2 flats has the first two: B♭ E♭.

Reading a signature fast

  • Sharp keys: the last sharp is a half step below the key name. Last sharp G♯ → A major.
  • Flat keys: the second-to-last flat is the key name. B♭ E♭ A♭ → E♭ major.
  • Memorize two: C has nothing, F has one flat (B♭).
  • Each step clockwise on the circle adds a sharp; each step counter-clockwise adds a flat.
  • Enharmonic keys at the bottom always add up to 12: B (5♯) = C♭ (7♭), F♯ (6♯) = G♭ (6♭), C♯ (7♯) = D♭ (5♭).
